## T2D-D02 · A pending suspicion ruling hides a same-ID vendor conflict

**Exit (a), I-2.** On November 17 AAPL falls from $100 to $50 with no listed split. The founder answers the resulting price suspicion with a 2:1 split. On November 18 the splits endpoint succeeds and returns **3:2 and 5:4**, both effective November 17. The dividends endpoint fails. The real daily builder retains both split rows and marks coverage failed. The close keeps 99.970008 shares but records neither vendor payload as a conflict. On November 19 both endpoints recover with no rows. The idea, comparison and book now show **199.940016 AAPL shares at full quality**. A conflict ruling choosing the reported 3:2 returns 404. The founder had ruled on the price move before either vendor ratio arrived. (Rev1:24, Rev1:33–46, mandateUniverseSnapshot.js:443–459, mandateUniverseSnapshot.js:556–589, holdRulings.js:305–313, Run:93.)

With coverage on November 18, the system applies 2:1 immediately, then records the two vendor ratios as a revision of that outcome. Choosing 3:2 returns 422. Without the earlier suspicion ruling, the same two incompatible vendor ratios create a selectable conflict and the count stays unchanged. An agreeing single 2:1 row applies once. FQ-6 correctly limits *reconfirmation* of an already settled outcome. The error occurs earlier, when the cash or shares can first move on an ambiguous ID. (Build:893, Build:1069–1074, ledger.js:996–1005, holdRulings.js:174–195, Run:93.)

**Small fix.** When composing the rows for a pending suspicion split, check same-ID vendor payloads before replacing them. Preserve an agreeing duplicate’s single application. If the vendor rows disagree with one another or the pending ratio, pass the disagreement into normal conflict recording, preserve it across coverage failure, and require `resolve_hold(conflict)` for that action ID. The current replacement removes the rows before conflict detection, and the observation path only extends a record that already exists.
