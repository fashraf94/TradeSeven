Cathie Wood, Ross Gerber Snap Up Shares Of Battered Snap

By Markets Desk · Yahoo Finance May 24, 2022, 2:41 PM ET

Two of Snap Inc.'s (NYSE: SNAP) most vocal bulls added to their positions on Tuesday after the social media company's surprise profit warning sent shares to a multi-year low. Cathie Wood's ARK Investment Management and Gerber Kawasaki Wealth and Investment Management each scooped up additional Snap stock as the broader Nasdaq sold off on growth concerns.

Wood's flagship ARK Innovation ETF (ARKK) and ARK Next Generation Internet ETF (ARKW) added a combined 1.6 million shares of Snap, according to ARK's daily trade disclosures. The purchases brought Snap's weighting in ARKK back above 2%, even as the stock closed down more than 43% on the session.

"We continue to view Snap as one of the most attractive social-platform franchises in our coverage," Wood wrote in a note to investors. ARK's broader portfolio also includes significant positions in Tesla (TSLA), Coinbase (COIN), and Roku (ROKU) — names Wood has repeatedly argued are early in their addressable-market expansion despite recent drawdowns.

Ross Gerber, CEO of Gerber Kawasaki, said on Twitter that he added to his Snap position in personal and firm accounts. "The long-term story hasn't changed," Gerber said. "Direct-response ads will recover, AR is still a massive opportunity, and Snap's user base in the 13-34 demographic remains best-in-class." Gerber Kawasaki's published holdings also include Block (SQ) and UiPath (PATH), both of which the firm has called out as beneficiaries of the same secular trends.

Snap's profit warning, filed in an 8-K after Monday's close, said the macroeconomic environment had "deteriorated further and faster than anticipated." CEO Evan Spiegel told employees the company would slow hiring and re-evaluate operating expenses through year-end. The disclosure dragged the broader digital-advertising complex lower, with Meta Platforms (META) and Pinterest (PINS) both selling off in sympathy.

Analysts at MoffettNathanson called the print "the canary in the coal mine for second-half ad spend" but said Snap's valuation, now trading at roughly 4x forward revenue, has begun to price in materially lower growth assumptions. Snap shares ended Tuesday's session at $12.79, the lowest close since the company's 2017 IPO.
